A C-90 holds forty-five minutes a side, and it does not care what you think of that. You cannot skip. You can fast-forward, but fast-forwarding is a physical act with a physical cost: the motor whines, the counter spins, and you land somewhere approximate. The tape has an opinion about sequence, and the opinion is *yours*, fixed at the moment you pressed record.

## The paradox

Streaming gives you every song ever made and takes away the reason to choose one. The tape gives you ninety minutes and makes every one of them a decision. That is the paradox the algorithm cannot resolve: scarcity produced by the medium is not a bug in the medium. It is the entire aesthetic proposition.

People did not rediscover tapes because they sound good. They do not, particularly. They rediscovered them because a tape is a *commitment device*. A mix is a letter, an object you hand to someone, with a tracklist in handwriting that will still be legible when the account that hosted the playlist has been sunset.

> Hiss is not the absence of signal. Hiss is the sound of the medium admitting it exists.
> <cite>— Marginal note, dubbing log, 2019</cite>

## Type II, chrome, and the sacred fragility

Chrome dioxide tape, the Type II stock with the extra notch on the shell, was the underground's default because it handled loud, dense material without smearing. It was also fragile. Unspool one in a car door and you have lost the record. This fragility was not tolerated; it was *cherished*. A tape that can die is a tape you take care of, and taking care of things is the opposite of streaming.

## The 45-minute argument

A side of tape is roughly the length of an attention span that has not been trained to expect interruption. Mixes made for cassette have shape: an opening, a sag around minute twenty where you put the song that needs patience, a last track chosen because the leader will click in exactly as it fades. Nobody sequences a playlist that way, because a playlist has no end.

The tape hiss paradox, then: the format that carries the least information carries the most intention. We are keeping the hiss.
